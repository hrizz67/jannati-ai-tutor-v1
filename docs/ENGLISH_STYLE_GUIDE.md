# English Style Guide

Project: Jannati AI Tutor v2.0

This guide sets the permanent English style baseline for Year 2 Malaysian learners.
It is a reference document only. It does not change any question bank content.

## 1. Year 2 classroom English

- Use short, clear sentences.
- Keep instructions direct and easy to understand.
- Prefer one idea per sentence.
- Use everyday classroom vocabulary.
- Keep questions friendly and simple.

## 2. CEFR-aligned vocabulary

- Prefer beginner-friendly words.
- Avoid rare, technical, or abstract vocabulary.
- Use familiar nouns, verbs, colours, places, and actions.
- Keep sentence patterns predictable.

## 3. Malaysian classroom teacher tone

- Sound warm, encouraging, and calm.
- Use a teacher-like voice.
- Guide the learner gently.
- Avoid robotic or machine-like phrasing.

## 4. Sentence length guidelines

- Question stem: ideally 4 to 14 words before the blank or target prompt.
- Hint: 3 to 8 words when possible.
- Explanation: 3 to 10 words when possible.
- Avoid long multi-clause instructions in learner-facing text.

## 5. Preferred instructions

Use clear, familiar patterns such as:

1. Complete the sentence.
2. Choose the correct answer.
3. Choose the correct word.
4. Read the sentence.
5. Look at the picture.
6. Circle the correct word.
7. Tick the correct answer.
8. Fill in the blank.
9. Match the words.
10. Find the correct word.
11. Write the missing word.
12. Underline the correct answer.
13. Pick the best word.
14. Read and answer.
15. Look and choose.
16. Choose the best word.
17. Read carefully.
18. Complete the word.
19. Choose one word.
20. Answer the question.
21. Select the correct word.
22. Read and choose.
23. Find the missing word.
24. Say the answer.
25. Check the sentence.
26. Read the short text.
27. Match the sentence.
28. Choose the correct option.
29. Look at the sentence.
30. Fill the missing word.

## 6. Hint style

- Hints should guide, not reveal.
- Use short prompts like:
  - Think about the animal.
  - Choose the place.
  - Look for the action word.
- Avoid giving the answer directly.

## 7. Explanation style

- Keep explanations short and factual.
- Explain why the answer fits.
- Use simple classroom English.
- Avoid dictionary-style definitions when a short teaching reason is enough.

## 8. Encouragement style

- Use positive, supportive language.
- Keep encouragement short and specific.
- Prefer phrases such as:
  - Good try.
  - Well done.
  - Keep going.
  - Try again.
  - Great effort.

## 9. Forbidden wording

- Robotic instructions that sound machine-generated.
- Overly formal classroom language.
- Long academic explanations for simple Year 2 tasks.
- Repeated instruction openers used too often without variation.
- Unclear or unnatural phrasing.

## 10. Good vs poor examples

Good:

- Complete the sentence.
- Choose the correct word.
- Think about the animal.
- A cat says meow.

Poor:

- In Simple Sentences, choose one word: Complete the sentence.
- Read and fill in the blank: Complete the sentence.
- Consider the most appropriate lexical item.
- The animal performs the sound meow.
